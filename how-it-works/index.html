How it works · for beginners

What the satellite sees, and what it can't

A short guide for anyone new to this. No background needed.

The short version

What the satellite sees

  • A photo about every 5 days. Free European photo satellites pass over Nilabadi and take a photo of the area about every 5 days. FarmBot downloads these photos for free.
  • Each photo is made of 10 m squares. One square is about the size of a small house plot. The satellite can't see single plants, only the average colour of each square.
  • Greenness means a healthy, leafy crop. Healthy leaves reflect a lot of invisible light that cameras on the satellite can measure. From that, FarmBot works out a greenness score from 0 to 1. A bare field scores low. A thick green paddy crop scores high.
  • Watching greenness week by week shows the crop's life. The score rises when the crop starts growing (green-up), is highest when it is fully grown (peak), and falls as it ripens and is harvested. These are the crop life stages.
  • It also sees open water. A water score shows how much of the fishing pond has water on its surface.

Be careful

What it can't see

  • Through clouds. In the monsoon the sky is often cloudy. Cloudy parts of a photo are thrown away (cloud removal). In July and August 2026 there were only one or two clear photos, so there are gaps just when the crop is planted. A radar satellite that sees through clouds may fill these gaps later.
  • Pests and diseases, directly. Insects are far too small, and early disease doesn't change the colour of a 10 m square. Pest warnings will come from the weather (heat, rain and humidity that pests like). Later, sensors, insect traps and cameras on the fields will add ground checks. See Pest alerts.
  • How much grain you'll get. Greenness only lets FarmBot compare this year with last year. It can't give a harvest weight in tonnes.
  • Soil and rain on your own field. Soil and weather numbers on this site are computer estimates for a wider area. There is no rain gauge or soil test on the farm yet.
  • Exact field edges. The outlines were traced by hand, not surveyed. Areas are approximate.

FarmBot is an experiment. It uses satellites only for now. Always trust what you see in the field first.

Word list

Every term on the site, explained

In alphabetical order. The technical name is shown small, in case you see it elsewhere.

10 m square
The smallest square in a satellite photo, 10 m by 10 m (100 square metres). A field of 1 hectare holds about 100 of them. Squares on a field edge often mix in bunds or the next field.Technical name: Pixel
Cloud removal
Each photo comes with a map of which squares are cloud, cloud shadow or haze. Those squares are thrown away. A date counts only if most of the field is clear.Technical name: Cloud mask
Crop life stages
The crop's stages as seen from space: green-up (the crop starts growing and the field turns green), peak (the greenest point), and ripening (leaves turn yellow, so greenness falls before harvest).Technical name: Phenology
Estimate
Worked out by a computer weather or soil model for a big area, not measured on these fields. Good for trends, not for exact amounts.Technical name: Modelled
Experimental
A first try. It may change once ground checks or more seasons are available.Technical name: Experimental
Greenness score
How green and leafy the crop looks from space, on a scale from 0 to 1. Healthy, thick leaves reflect a lot of invisible near-infrared light, and the score uses that. On this site, under 0.4 means a dry or empty field, 0.4 to 0.65 a young crop, and 0.65 or more a fully green crop.Technical name: NDVI (Normalised Difference Vegetation Index)
Half-way green date
The rough date when the field got half-way from its dry-season low to its green peak.Technical name: Green-up (50% point)
Haze-corrected photos
The version of the photos that has been cleaned of haze in the air, so colours are closer to what the ground really looks like.Technical name: Level-2A (L2A)
Hectare
A unit of land area: 100 m by 100 m, or 10,000 square metres. 1 hectare is about 2.47 acres.Technical name: ha
Humidity
How much moisture is in the air, in per cent. 100% is fully saturated, like fog or dew.Technical name: RH (relative humidity)
Land-use map
A free 2021 map by the European Space Agency that labels every 10 m square as crop fields, trees, grass, buildings or water. FarmBot uses it to leave out trees, buildings and water near the plots.Technical name: ESA WorldCover 2021
Lining up a screenshot
Fitting a phone map screenshot onto real map positions by matching roads, ponds and tree clumps.Technical name: Georeferencing
Metre map grid
The map grid FarmBot's map files use. It measures position in metres, so areas and distances come out right. Readers of this site don't need it.Technical name: UTM zone 44N (EPSG:32644)
Millimetres of rain
How deep the water would be on flat ground if none soaked in or ran off. 25 mm in a month is very little. A heavy monsoon month here can bring several hundred.Technical name: mm
Monsoon crop season
The main rain-fed paddy season, roughly June to November.Technical name: Kharif
NASA weather estimates
Free daily weather estimates from NASA, the United States space agency, on a grid about 50 km wide.Technical name: NASA POWER
Online photo catalogue
The free online library from which FarmBot downloads the satellite photos. It lists every photo by date and place.Technical name: STAC catalogue, Microsoft Planetary Computer
Open-Meteo
A free weather service. FarmBot uses its rebuilt past weather for rain, soil wetness and water pulled out by sun and wind.Technical name: Open-Meteo historical weather
Pheromone trap
A small trap that uses a scent to attract the male moths of one pest. Counting the catch shows when that pest is active.Technical name: Pheromone trap
Photo satellite
A pair of free European satellites that photograph the area about every 5 days. They take photos in visible and invisible light. Each photo is made of 10 m by 10 m squares.Technical name: Sentinel-2
Radar echo
How much of the radar signal bounces back. Still water reflects it away, so a flooded field looks dark. It gets brighter as the crop grows.Technical name: Backscatter
Radar satellite
A free European radar satellite. Radar sends its own signal and sees through clouds, so it works in the monsoon. It shows standing water well. FarmBot has not used it yet.Technical name: Sentinel-1
Ripening
The stage when paddy leaves dry and turn yellow-brown as the grain fills. The greenness score falls steadily.Technical name: Senescence
Seen by satellite
Read directly from satellite photos of these fields.Technical name: Satellite-observed
Smoothed line
Single photos jump up and down because of haze and light. The smoothed line takes the middle value of all photos within 10 days either side of each date, so the trend is easier to see.Technical name: 20-day running median
Soil wetness
How much of the soil is water. It is shown for the top 7 cm and for 28 to 100 cm deep. These are computer estimates, not measured on the farm.Technical name: Soil moisture
Spread band
The shaded band around a line shows where the middle half of the field's squares fell on each date.Technical name: 25th to 75th percentile
Water pulled out by sun and wind
How much water the sun, heat and wind pull out of wet soil and plants each day, in millimetres. If this is more than the rain, the field is drying out.Technical name: ET0 (reference evapotranspiration)
Water score
How much open water or wetness shows in a spot. Above 0 means open water. There are two versions. They use slightly different colours of light and give slightly different pond sizes.Technical name: NDWI and MNDWI (water indexes)
Weak patch
A part of a field that stays less green than the rest, year after year. It could mean poor soil, waterlogging or pests, and is a place to go and look.Technical name: Weak zone
Weather rebuilt by computer
Past weather worked out by a computer from many weather stations and satellites, on a grid about 9 to 11 km wide.Technical name: Reanalysis
Who's who in the sources
ICAR is the Indian Council of Agricultural Research, the government's farm research body. NRRI is the National Rice Research Institute in Cuttack (its old name was CRRI). NCIPM is ICAR's National Centre for Integrated Pest Management. NICRA is ICAR's national project on climate-resilient farming. IIRR is the Indian Institute of Rice Research. IRRI is the International Rice Research Institute. IMD is the India Meteorological Department, the weather office. IPM means integrated pest management: scouting, traps, and spraying only when needed. LGD is the government's Local Government Directory of villages.Technical name: ICAR, NRRI (CRRI), NCIPM, NICRA, IIRR, IRRI, IMD, IPM, LGD
Winter crop season
The dry winter season after the paddy harvest. Crops such as green gram, black gram or mustard can be grown then if there is water.Technical name: Rabi
World soil map
A free world soil map from ISRIC (the International Soil Reference and Information Centre). It estimates soil type on a 250 m grid. It is a prediction, not a test of this soil.Technical name: ISRIC SoilGrids 2.0
Year-to-year match score
A number from -1 to 1 that says whether the same squares were low in both years. Near 1 means the same squares were low both times. Near 0, or below 0, means no pattern.Technical name: Spearman rank correlation
Yield comparison score
A score comparing how green this year's crop was against last year's, over the same dates. It is not a harvest weight in tonnes. 1.00 means the same as last year. Above 1 means greener. Below 1 means less green.Technical name: Relative yield index
