How

How the numbers are made

  • The plots. Outlines were traced from the map screenshots on which you marked the fields. The Fishing Pond plot (2.49 ha (6.2 acres)) was redrawn as one outline from your close-up screenshot on 31 Oct 2026. The Temple plot is one block north of the road, opposite the Agnigangamma Temple (6.31 ha (15.6 acres)); its outline was redrawn from your corrected screenshot on 1 Nov 2026. The Panchayat Pond north of the Fishing Pond plot was traced from the close-up aerial photo (0.81 ha (2.00 acres)). Each screenshot was lined up with close-up aerial photos by matching roads, ponds and tree clumps, to within about 1 m. The Fishing Pond outline follows the bunds you can see. The temple outline follows your hand-drawn line and stays off the road. They are not surveyed boundaries.
  • Crop greenness. FarmBot uses every cloud-free satellite photo from 1 Oct 2025 to 23 Oct 2026. For each one it takes the middle greenness score of the 10 m squares whose centre is inside each outline. Squares that the land-use map labels trees, buildings or water are dropped. A photo only counts when at least 60% of those squares are clear of cloud. The values are then smoothed. From the smoothed line FarmBot reads the greenest point, the dates it fell below 0.7 and 0.5, the dry-season low, and the half-way green date.
  • Pond water. Squares inside the traced Panchayat Pond outline (plus 10 m) where the water score is above 0 count as open water, and are added up in hectares, on photos where at least 80% of that area is clear of cloud. Water under floating weeds is not counted.
  • Locations. The pond was found by matching a map screenshot to water seen by the satellite. The temple position comes from a public map listing (plus code X768+5R7), checked against photos and OpenStreetMap. The lined-up screenshot puts its pin within about 6 m of that point.
  • Weekly update. A script builds the site from the data folders. It can be re-run each week after new photos are downloaded.
  • Advice and checks (early versions). Yield comparison: the middle greenness of each plot on cloud-free photos (at least 60% clear) in the same 1–23 October window, 2025 against 2026. Harvest heads-up: the weekly change in the smoothed greenness. Weak patches: each square's greenness minus the field's middle value, taken over each October's cloud-free photos. Only squares fully inside the outline are tested. Both plots leave out a 10 m strip round the edge. A square counts as weak if it is in the lowest 20% in both seasons. Winter water: each crop's water use from the drying by sun and wind and its stage (world crop tables), minus the part of the winter rain that reaches the roots, divided by 50% to cover losses on the way; compared with the pond's latest open-water area for each metre of usable depth, after the pond's own drying. 25 winters of Open-Meteo weather. Neighbours: paddy within 2 km found by the dry-season, radar-flooding and October tests, grouped into 50 m patches, each given the same October score.
  • Radar. Radar satellite passes about every 12 days (53 passes, 6 Jan 2025 to 23 Oct 2026). The darkest monsoon pass inside each outline is taken as the flooding for transplanting, so dates are good to about ±12 days. The road-side strip of the Fishing Pond plot (within 50 m of the Panchayat Pond) is compared with the rest of the plot on every pass.
  • Pest weather check (early version, untested). The rules are copied unchanged from the government and rice-research documents listed below (who's who). Each week's weather (recent weather plus the Open-Meteo forecast) is checked against them, only at the crop stages when each pest does damage. They have not been checked against pest counts in Koraput.
  • Weekly advice. Brings together the crop stage, the pest check, heavy rain days (40 mm or more) and dry spells from the forecast, and the newest official advice from KVK Koraput, IMD and NRRI. Links are checked before each build. No pesticide doses.
  • Harvest heads-up. Each week new photos are checked for cloud. The latest clear greenness is compared with the same week last year, and last year's drop dates are shifted by the radar planting dates to give a rough window.
Technical details (for specialists)
  • Photos: Sentinel-2 Level-2A (haze-corrected), from the Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC catalogue, tiles 44QQF/44QQG. Map grid: UTM zone 44N (EPSG:32644).
  • Bands are colours of light: B03 green, B04 red, B08 near-infrared (invisible), B11 short-wave infrared (invisible). NDVI = (B08−B04)/(B08+B04). NDWI = (B03−B08)/(B03+B08). MNDWI = (B03−B11)/(B03+B11).
  • Clear squares for NDVI are the scene classes vegetation, bare soil and water. A −1000 offset is applied for processing baseline 4 and later.
  • Crop series: median NDVI inside the outline, 20-day running median, peak, 0.7 and 0.5 crossings, dry-season floor and 50% amplitude green-up. Only WorldCover class 40 (cropland).
  • Lining up screenshots: SIFT keypoint matching with a rotation-scale-shift fit and outlier rejection, against Esri World Imagery.
  • Weak zones: Spearman rank correlation of per-pixel anomalies between Octobers 2025 and 2026; bottom-20% cut in both seasons.
  • Radar: Sentinel-1 RTC (radiometrically terrain corrected) from the Planetary Computer, one descending orbit, median VV and VH backscatter in dB per outline; dip = lowest VV+VH sum between 1 Jun and 30 Sep.
  • Pest check: standard meteorological weeks; insect rules use the previous week's weather; leaf wetness estimated from rain and RH ≥ 90%. Open-Meteo forecast for the coming 16 days.

Limits

  • For now FarmBot uses satellites only. Always trust what you see in the field first.
  • The field outlines are traced by hand, not surveyed. They were drawn from the marked maps and lined up with close-up aerial photos (to within about 1–2 m). The edges are right to about 5–10 m.
  • The photos are coarse. Each 10 m square is one dot of colour. The Fishing Pond plot holds only about 250 of these squares, and the ones on its edge mix in bunds and the next field. So detail inside a plot is only a rough hint.
  • Clouds hide the monsoon. In July and August there were only one or two cloud-free photos. A radar satellite that sees through cloud now gives rough planting dates, good to about ±12 days.
  • Soil and weather numbers are computer estimates, not measurements on these fields. Areas are approximate.

Weather

How the weather numbers are made

  • NASA POWER daily weather is worked out on a grid about 50 km wide, so it describes the wider area, not one field.
  • Open-Meteo past weather is rebuilt by computer on a finer grid, about 9–11 km wide. Its forecast covers the coming 16 days.
  • Both are estimates, good for trends rather than exact amounts. The last month in the table may be incomplete.

Pest alerts

Why the pest levels are not yet checked here

The insect rules were worked out from light-trap counts at Raipur and other places, and the disease rules from research write-ups. None has been checked in Koraput's hill climate, so the levels may warn too little or too much. Treat a high level as "go and look", not as proof of a pest. One season of field notes (date, field, count) would let the rules be tested.

Winter crop water: why the real numbers may differ

  • The depth and the water already used are assumptions. A measured depth and a rough count of pumping hours would replace them.
  • Leftover soil wetness after paddy is not counted, so the land that can be watered is probably more. The rice institute says it can supply 55–80% of a winter crop's water in rice fallows.
  • Water seeping out of the pond bottom is not known. It lowers the numbers.
  • The crop water needs come from world crop tables, not from Koraput trials. Black gram uses the green gram values. Half of the pumped water is assumed lost on the way and in the field (open channels and flooding small basins).
  • If the Fishing Pond plot is harvested in early December, sowing could slip to mid-December. Mustard is best sown in September–October, but late sowing from mid-November to mid-December is common in eastern India after a late paddy harvest (Rice Knowledge Bank, Odisha).

Village page

How the village page is made

  • Crop stage (updated weekly). FarmBot maps paddy within 2 km of the two followed plots (about 1,981 ha, more than the village itself), using the same paddy test as the neighbour comparison. On the latest clear photo each paddy square is called still green, turning golden (below 85% of its greenest this season) or cut or bare (greenness below 0.40). The test finds only about 59–63% of the two followed plots, so it undercounts paddy. The village boundary is not available, so the map can't be cut to the village and gives no Nilabadi total. Satellite only, not checked on the ground.
  • Pest risk. The same weekly weather check as Nilabadi pest alerts: the highest level at the followed plots, for the crop stage most paddy is at. Not yet checked against field counts here.
  • Weather. The Open-Meteo forecast at the two followed plots: heavy rain days (40 mm or more) and dry spells.
  • Photos and land cover. The photos on the village page cover a 2.2 km square around the village. They are dated and are not redrawn each week. The land-cover sentence comes from a free 2021 land-use map made from 10 m squares, so it doesn't show later changes.
  • Census figures. From the 2011 Census and the government village list (Local Government Directory), via Odiapedia. They are from 2011 and may have changed. Village code 428775.
