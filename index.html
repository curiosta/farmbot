What FarmBot will do

Three jobs, in order

FarmBot is a farming helper for two paddy plots in Nilabadi. Today it uses free satellite photos and computer weather estimates. Next paddy season it will add sensors, insect traps and cameras on the fields.

Job 1 · being built

Pest and disease warnings

Early warnings when the weather suits paddy pests and diseases. These include stem borer, gall midge, leaf folder, brown planthopper, blast, sheath blight and bacterial leaf blight. Every rule comes from government and rice-research sources. No warnings are shown yet, because the rules must first be checked here. →

Job 2

Weekly advice

A short note each week for each plot. It will cover the crop's stage, rain against drying, pest risk, and links to the latest official farm advice for Koraput. →

Job 3 · experimental

Is this year's crop better or worse?

A yield comparison score compares how green the crop is with last season. It is a comparison, not tonnes of grain. On 1–23 October the pond fields were less green than in 2025 and the temple plot greener. This year's crop is still growing, so this may change. →

Also planned

When will it be ready to harvest?

Each week FarmBot checks whether greenness has started to fall. It hasn't started falling yet.

Parts of a field that stay less green two years running. They also show where the first sensors should go.

Radar that sees through cloud

A radar satellite can show flooding, waterlogging and transplanting dates even in the monsoon. This will help the pest warnings.

How much pulse or mustard the pond could water after the paddy harvest. We still need to know the pond's depth.

Please read with care

  • FarmBot is an experiment. For now it uses satellites only. Always trust what you see in the field first.
  • The field outlines are traced by hand, not surveyed. They were drawn from the maps you marked and lined up with close-up aerial photos (to within about 1–2 m). The edges are right to about 5–10 m.
  • The photos are coarse. Each 10 m square is one dot of colour. The two pond fields hold only about 100 and 40 of these squares, and many mix in bunds and the next field. So detail inside a field is only a rough hint.
  • Clouds hide the monsoon. In July and August there were only one or two cloud-free photos. So the satellite can't tell when transplanting happened.
  • Soil and weather numbers are computer estimates, not measurements on these fields. Areas are approximate.
What gets updated. Only the two marked plots (the pond fields and the temple plot) are updated each week. The village page and the old snapshot from 31 Oct 2026 are one-time pages and are not updated. Later, a wider view of other fields or villages might become a paid service for others. That is only an idea for now.
