A farm helper for the paddy fields of Nilabadi

FarmBot is a farm helper for the paddy fields of Nilabadi village, Bandhugaon, Koraput, Odisha. Today it follows two plots closely: the Fishing Pond plot and the Temple plot (by the Agnigangamma Temple). There is also a first, one-time view of paddy across the village. More fields will be added over time. It uses free satellite photos now; ground sensors and cameras come later. Everything is explained in plain words.

What FarmBot will do

Three jobs, in order

FarmBot is a farming helper for the paddy fields of Nilabadi. Today it follows two plots closely, using free satellite photos and computer weather estimates. Next paddy season it will add sensors, insect traps and cameras on the fields.

Job 1 · working draft, untested

Pest and disease weather check

Each week: does the weather suit stem borer, gall midge, leaf folder, brown planthopper, blast, sheath blight or bacterial leaf blight? This week: Blast high at the Fishing Pond plot. The rules are not yet checked against pest counts here, so ask KVK Koraput before spraying. →

Job 2 · working draft

Weekly advice

A short note each week for each plot, on its plot page: the crop's stage, pest weather, heavy rain days and dry spells, and links to the newest official farm advice for Koraput. →

Job 3 · experimental

Is this year's crop better or worse?

A yield comparison score compares how green the crop is with last season. It is a comparison, not tonnes of grain. The Fishing Pond plot looks a little behind last year, mostly because it was planted later. A fair comparison is possible only after harvest. The Temple plot is greener than last year. →

Please read with care

  • FarmBot is an experiment. For now it uses satellites only. Always trust what you see in the field first.
  • The field outlines are traced by hand, not surveyed. They were drawn from the maps you marked and lined up with close-up aerial photos (to within about 1–2 m). The edges are right to about 5–10 m.
  • The photos are coarse. Each 10 m square is one dot of colour. The Fishing Pond plot holds only about 250 of these squares, and the ones on its edge mix in bunds and the next field. So detail inside a plot is only a rough hint.
  • Clouds hide the monsoon. In July and August there were only one or two cloud-free photos. A radar satellite that sees through cloud now gives rough planting dates, good to about ±12 days.
  • Soil and weather numbers are computer estimates, not measurements on these fields. Areas are approximate.
What gets updated. Only the plots followed closely (the Fishing Pond plot and the Temple plot) are updated each week. The village page and the old snapshot from 31 Oct 2026 are one-time pages and are not updated. Later, a wider view of other fields or villages might become a paid service for others.
