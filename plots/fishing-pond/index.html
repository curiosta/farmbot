Fishing Pond plot

One paddy plot traced from your marked map, next to the fishing pond. This week's advice, satellite photos, greenness maps, the crop's year month by month, a weak-patch check, the two ponds and a soil estimate.

Weekly advice · written 31 Oct 2026

This week: 29 Oct – 4 Nov 2026

Crop stage: greenest stage (ear forming to flowering). High weather risk: Blast; Watch: Sheath blight. See the pest alerts for the reasons.

What to do this week

  • Harvest: not yet. The last clear satellite picture (23 Oct) still showed a green crop (greenness 0.89). Rough harvest window 15 Nov–05 Dec (estimate from last year plus radar planting dates; planted about 3–4 weeks later than last year (radar), so likely in the later part of the window). Start checking ears from about 10 Nov: harvest when 80–85% of grains are straw-coloured and hard.
  • Rain: heavy-rain days expected Wed 04 Nov (78 mm), Thu 05 Nov (91 mm). Before them, open the field outlets so low corners drain; do not cut, thresh or dry grain outdoors on rainy days (IMD advice).
  • Road-side strip: the plot has a second pond just across the road on its north side. After the heavy rain, walk the strip next to the road and check it drains; standing water there can delay cutting.
  • Storms/wind: if plants fall over, tie 4–5 hills together so ears stay off the water (IMD advice).
  • Drier days in the forecast (under 2 mm rain a day), useful for field work: 10 Nov–13 Nov (4 days).
  • Check for Blast (HIGH weather risk): Walk the field and look at the neck just below the ear: a grey-brown ring there (neck blast) makes the ear go white and empty. Also look for eye-shaped spots with grey centres on leaves. If you find it, ask KVK Koraput or the agriculture officer what to do before buying any spray.
  • Keep an eye on Sheath blight (watch): Part the plants and look at the stems near the water line for grey-green oval patches with brown edges.
  • After harvest: dry grain in the sun to about 14% moisture before storing; 12% if keeping it as seed (NRRI advice).

Weather is a computer forecast (estimate). Pest levels come from the weather, not from insect counts. FarmBot gives no pesticide doses: ask KVK Koraput or the agriculture officer before any spraying.

Inside the plot · 10 m squares

Is any part of the plot always weaker? Experimental

Each 10 m square fully inside the outline (leaving out the edge row) is compared with the plot's middle value on the same day. This uses the cloud-free photos of 1–23 October, in both 2025 and 2026. A real weak patch would be low in both years.

Result: a possible weak patch

188 squares tested. 21 squares (about 0.21 ha) were in the lowest 20% in both years. That is 11% of the squares, against about 4% you'd expect by chance. Most are in the south-west part of the plot. Year-to-year match score: 0.34, a weak but real pattern.

Worth a walk to look for poor soil, standing water or pests in that part. The weak strip in the old snapshot is outside this plot (about 195 m away, none of it inside), so it says nothing about it. Technical name: weak-zone check with Spearman rank correlation

Water on two sides · seen by satellite

Two ponds next to the plot

The plot has water on two sides: the fishing pond about 320 m to the south-west, and a second pond just across the road to the north. FarmBot now tracks the open water in both, on every cloud-free photo from 1 Oct 2025 to 23 Oct 2026. Seen by satellite

What the second pond means for the plot

  • A possible backup water source. It is right next to the plot, but only part of it is open water: 0.25 ha on 23 Oct, at most 0.40 ha, out of a 0.81 ha outline. The rest seems covered by weeds or reeds.
  • Some extra risk of waterlogging on the road side in heavy rain. But the radar does not show that strip staying wetter than the rest of the plot (radar check).
  • Slightly more humid air near the water is likely, which suits some leaf diseases. This is not measured on the plot.

The fishing pond

Open water 1.12 ha on 23 Oct, at most 1.58 ha. It held water all through the dry season.

The fishing pond over a longer period

The main fishing pond is about 475 m from the plot's centre. FarmBot measured the open water within 150 m of the pond's centre, using the water score (above 0 means water). It stayed between 0.9 and 1.6 ha on every cloud-free photo from Nov 2025 to Jun 2026.

The second version of the water score suggests the pond shrank from about 1.5 ha (Dec–Jan middle value) to about 1.0 ha (Mar–May middle value). This is the water's surface area only. The satellite can't see depth or how much water there is. Seen by satellite Technical name: NDWI and MNDWI

Please read with care

  • FarmBot is an experiment. For now it uses satellites only. Always trust what you see in the field first.
  • The field outlines are traced by hand, not surveyed. They were drawn from the maps you marked and lined up with close-up aerial photos (to within about 1–2 m). The edges are right to about 5–10 m.
  • The photos are coarse. Each 10 m square is one dot of colour. The Fishing Pond plot holds only about 250 of these squares, and the ones on its edge mix in bunds and the next field. So detail inside a plot is only a rough hint.
  • Clouds hide the monsoon. In July and August there were only one or two cloud-free photos. A radar satellite that sees through cloud now gives rough planting dates, good to about ±12 days.
  • Soil and weather numbers are computer estimates, not measurements on these fields. Areas are approximate.
