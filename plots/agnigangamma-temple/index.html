Temple plot

A paddy plot traced from a marked map, opposite the Agnigangamma Temple. This week's advice, satellite photos, greenness maps, the crop's year month by month, a weak-patch check and a soil estimate.

Weekly advice · written 31 Oct 2026

This week: 29 Oct – 4 Nov 2026

Crop stage: grain filling / ripening. Watch: Blast. See the pest alerts for the reasons.

What to do this week

  • Harvest: not yet. The last clear satellite picture (23 Oct) still showed a green crop (greenness 0.87). Rough harvest window 15 Nov–05 Dec (estimate from last year plus radar planting dates; this plot was planted earlier than the Fishing Pond plot, so it is likely ready first). Start checking ears from about 10 Nov: harvest when 80–85% of grains are straw-coloured and hard.
  • Rain: heavy-rain days expected Wed 04 Nov (78 mm), Thu 05 Nov (91 mm). Before them, open the field outlets so low corners drain; do not cut, thresh or dry grain outdoors on rainy days (IMD advice).
  • Storms/wind: if plants fall over, tie 4–5 hills together so ears stay off the water (IMD advice).
  • Drier days in the forecast (under 2 mm rain a day), useful for field work: 10 Nov–13 Nov (4 days).
  • Keep an eye on Blast (watch): Walk the field and look at the neck just below the ear: a grey-brown ring there (neck blast) makes the ear go white and empty. Also look for eye-shaped spots with grey centres on leaves.
  • After harvest: dry grain in the sun to about 14% moisture before storing; 12% if keeping it as seed (NRRI advice).

Weather is a computer forecast (estimate). Pest levels come from the weather, not from insect counts. FarmBot gives no pesticide doses: ask KVK Koraput or the agriculture officer before any spraying.

Harvested or ripening?

Not harvested. On 23 Oct the plot's middle greenness was 0.87, and 100% of its squares scored above 0.6. That is as green as it gets. In 2025 its smoothed greenness fell below 0.7 about 10 days before the Fishing Pond plot.

Radar shows the plot was flooded for planting around 12 Aug 2026 (±12 days). The short green spell in late April and early May 2026 (middle value up to about 0.57) was before that, so it was most likely grass, weeds or a seedling nursery, not this year's crop.

Inside the plot · 10 m squares

Is any part of the plot always weaker? Experimental

Each 10 m square fully inside the outline (leaving out the edge row) is compared with the plot's middle value on the same day. This uses the cloud-free photos of 1–23 October, in both 2025 and 2026. A real weak patch would be low in both years.

Result: no weak patch

637 squares tested. Only 14 squares (2%) were in the lowest 20% in both years, fewer than the 4% you'd expect by chance. Year-to-year match score: -0.32. So FarmBot does not claim a weak patch. Technical name: weak-zone check with Spearman rank correlation

Please read with care

  • FarmBot is an experiment. For now it uses satellites only. Always trust what you see in the field first.
  • The field outlines are traced by hand, not surveyed. They were drawn from the maps you marked and lined up with close-up aerial photos (to within about 1–2 m). The edges are right to about 5–10 m.
  • The photos are coarse. Each 10 m square is one dot of colour. The Fishing Pond plot holds only about 250 of these squares, and the ones on its edge mix in bunds and the next field. So detail inside a plot is only a rough hint.
  • Clouds hide the monsoon. In July and August there were only one or two cloud-free photos. A radar satellite that sees through cloud now gives rough planting dates, good to about ±12 days.
  • Soil and weather numbers are computer estimates, not measurements on these fields. Areas are approximate.
