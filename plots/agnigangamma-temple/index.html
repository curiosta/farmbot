The exact plot

Traced from the map screenshot you marked. Each screenshot was fitted onto close-up aerial photos by matching roads, ponds and tree clumps, to within about 1 m (how this works). Only 10 m squares whose centre is inside the outline count towards the crop numbers.

Where it is. The plot is on the north side of the village road. The Agnigangamma Temple is opposite, on the south side (public map listing, plus code X768+5R7). The plot's centre is about 167 m north-north-west of the temple. The marked map was turned about 113°, so the top of that map is really west. A stream with a line of trees runs along the north-east edge. The earlier rough squares are kept in the old snapshot (not updated). Harvested or ripening?

Not harvested. On 23 Oct the plot's middle greenness was 0.87, and 100% of its squares scored above 0.6. That is as green as it gets. In 2025 its smoothed greenness fell below 0.7 about 10 days before the pond fields.

The satellite also shows a short green spell in late April and early May 2026 (middle value up to about 0.57). It was gone by mid-May. It could be a summer crop, weeds or a seedling nursery. Only someone on the ground can say.

Inside the field · 10 m squares

Is any part of the field always weaker? Experimental

Each 10 m square fully inside the outline is compared with the field's middle value on the same day. This uses the cloud-free photos of 1–23 October, in both 2025 and 2026. A real weak patch would be low in both years.

Result: no weak patch

  • Temple plot (north of the road): 637 squares tested. Year-to-year match score: -0.32. 14 square(s) were in the lowest 20% in both years (2%, against about 4% you'd expect by chance).

The squares that were low in one year are not the ones that were low in the other. So FarmBot does not claim a weak patch. Technical name: weak-zone check with Spearman rank correlation

Soil · from a world soil map

What the soil is probably like Estimate

A slightly sour (acidic) clay-loam (a fairly heavy, sticky soil), with pH 6.2–6.3. (pH 7 is neutral; lower is more sour.) The values are read at the centre of the plot. They come from a world soil map that predicts soil on 250 m squares, much bigger than the fields. Get a Soil Health Card or a lab test before deciding on fertiliser. Please read with care

  • FarmBot is an experiment. For now it uses satellites only. Always trust what you see in the field first.
  • The field outlines are traced by hand, not surveyed. They were drawn from the maps you marked and lined up with close-up aerial photos (to within about 1–2 m). The edges are right to about 5–10 m.
  • The photos are coarse. Each 10 m square is one dot of colour. The two pond fields hold only about 100 and 40 of these squares, and many mix in bunds and the next field. So detail inside a field is only a rough hint.
  • Clouds hide the monsoon. In July and August there were only one or two cloud-free photos. So the satellite can't tell when transplanting happened.
  • Soil and weather numbers are computer estimates, not measurements on these fields. Areas are approximate.
