Summary · written 7 Nov 2026 · updated each week

Fishing Pond plot at a glance

  • Crop now. Grain filling / ripening. On the last clear satellite photo (23 Oct) the crop was thick green, as green as the same week last year. No sign yet of the field turning golden.
  • This week. 5 Nov–11 Nov: no pest weather risk (a weather check, not a pest count). No heavy rain in the forecast for this plot's spot. Action: walk the field once. Ask KVK Koraput before any spray. Pest alerts for this plot →
  • Harvest. Rough window 15 Nov – 5 Dec (estimate). It was planted about 3–4 weeks later than last year, so likely in the later part of the window. Start checking the grain from about 10 Nov; cut when 80–85% of grains are straw-coloured and hard.
  • Against last year and the neighbours. In early October the crop was clearly less green than last year. Most paddy within 2 km was a little less green than last October. So against the neighbours this plot is about the same as them: it did better than about 5 in 10 of them. Much of this year's dip is the season, and the later planting.
  • Weak patch. A possible weak patch of about 0.21 ha in the south-west part was less green in both years. Worth a walk to look for poor soil, standing water or pests.
  • Water and a winter crop. Water already comes from the small Panchayat Pond across the road; it is the only usable source. It had 0.25 ha of open water on 23 Oct, out of a 0.81 ha outline mostly under weeds. At an assumed depth of about 1.5 m (like a swimming pool; not measured), and keeping back a quarter for the water already used (assumed), it could stretch to roughly 0.6–0.7 ha of black gram or green gram after the harvest (estimate), about a quarter of the 2.49 ha plot. Clearing some weeds is an option to get more water. The real depth still needs measuring.
  • Next steps. (1) Walk the south-west part and the road-side strip and check for neck blast. (2) Before December, measure the depth of the road-side Panchayat Pond with a marked pole, and decide whether to clear some weeds for more water.

Weekly advice · written 7 Nov 2026

This week: 5 Nov – 11 Nov 2026

Crop stage: grain filling / ripening (stage guessed from the calendar: last clear satellite picture is 16 days older than this week). No pest or disease above low this week. See the pest alerts for this plot for the reasons.

What to do this week

  • Harvest: not yet. The last clear satellite picture (23 Oct) still showed a thick green crop. Rough harvest window 15 Nov–05 Dec (estimate from last year plus radar planting dates; planted about 3–4 weeks later than last year (radar), so likely in the later part of the window). Start checking ears from about 10 Nov: harvest when 80–85% of grains are straw-coloured and hard.
  • Storms/wind: if plants fall over, tie 4–5 hills together so ears stay off the water (IMD advice).
  • Drier days in the forecast (under 2 mm rain a day), useful for field work: 07 Nov–20 Nov (14 days).
  • After harvest: dry grain in the sun to about 14% moisture before storing; 12% if keeping it as seed (NRRI advice).

Worth a walk to look for poor soil, standing water or pests in that part.

Water near the plot · seen by satellite

The two water bodies next to the plot

The Panchayat Pond is just across the road to the north. It belongs to the local Panchayat, and water is already drawn from it for the plot. It is the only water source FarmBot counts for a winter crop. FarmBot tracks its open water on every cloud-free photo from 1 Oct 2025 to 23 Oct 2026. Seen by satellite

The village fishing pond is about 320 m to the south-west. Its water can't be used for the plot, so it is shown for context only and its water is not tracked.

The Panchayat Pond

  • Small and weedy. Open water 0.25 ha on 23 Oct, at most 0.40 ha in the last year, out of a 0.81 ha outline. The rest seems covered by weeds or reeds. Clearing some weeds is an option to get more usable water.
  • Depth not measured. The winter-crop estimate assumes about 1.5 m (winter crop water).

Open water seen from space; water under floating weeds or reeds is not counted, and a narrow pond is undercounted at its edges.

What the water nearby means for the crop

  • Pest risk. Standing water nearby can raise the risk of planthoppers and some diseases. Look at the road-side strip when you walk the plot.
  • Some extra risk of waterlogging on the road side in heavy rain. But the radar does not show that strip staying wetter than the rest of the plot (radar check).
  • Slightly more humid air near the water is likely, which suits some leaf diseases. This is not measured on the plot.
