Job 1 · Pest and disease alerts

Early warning for paddy pests

Warnings based on the weather, for the two plots, using only rules from official sources. This draft shows how it will work. No warnings are shown yet.

Can the satellite see pests? No. Insects are far too small, and early disease doesn't change the colour of a 10 m square. So warnings will come from the weather that pests and diseases like. Later, sensors, insect traps and cameras on the fields will add real counts. Read more.

No warnings yet

The weather rules above were worked out from light-trap counts at Raipur and other places. None has been checked in Koraput's hill climate. FarmBot will not show a risk level for these plots until a rule has been checked against what people find in the fields here. Until then, this page only shows how it will work.

How the warnings will work

From weather to a warning, step by step

1 · Weather

Forecast and recent weather

Hourly temperature, humidity, rain, wind and sunshine for the plots, from a forecast service at first Estimate. These are turned into the weekly values the rules use: day high, night low, morning and evening humidity, weekly rain, and sunshine.

2 · Wet leaves

Hours the leaves stay wet

Blast needs leaves to stay wet for more than 10 hours [2]. Until a leaf-wetness sensor is put in, wet hours are estimated from rain and very damp air, and marked as an estimate.

3 · Crop stage

Crop stage from the satellite

The greenness score, and later a radar satellite, show the crop's stage: transplanting, new shoots forming, rice heads coming out, ripening. Each pest is only checked at the stages when it does damage.

4 · Rules

Count how many conditions are met

For each pest, count how many of the listed weather conditions the coming week meets, as the pest centre's rules do ([1]). More than three of five = high risk. Three = medium risk.

5 · Satellite check

"Less green than expected" flag

Separately, a plot gets a flag when its greenness falls below its own recent trend, or below nearby fields on the same day, at a time when it shouldn't be ripening. The flag means "go and look". It is not a diagnosis. Seen by satellite

6 · On the farm, later

Sensors, traps and cameras

A weather station and a leaf-wetness sensor on the plot will replace the computer estimates. Pheromone traps, with a camera that counts the moths, will give the stem-borer and leaf-folder counts. The rice institute's rule of 4–5 moths per trap needs these counts [3].

Warnings will point to official advice. When a risk is high, the warning will link to the latest advice from the National Rice Research Institute and the India Meteorological Department (the weather office) for Koraput [3] [9]. FarmBot will not suggest its own pesticide doses. Check with the local agriculture extension officer before any spraying.
