No risk scores yet

The weather rules above were fitted to light-trap data at Raipur and other study sites. None has been checked against Koraput's hill climate. FarmBot will not publish a risk level for these plots until a rule has been checked against field scouting here. Until then this page shows the method only.

How the alerts will work

A weather-driven risk model, step by step

1 · Weather in

Forecast and recent weather

Hourly temperature, humidity, rain, wind and sunshine for the plots, from a forecast service at first Modelled. They are rolled up into the weekly values the rules use (Tmax, Tmin, morning and evening RH, weekly rain, sunshine).

2 · Leaf wetness

Wet-leaf hours

Blast needs leaves wet for over 10 hours [2]. Until a leaf-wetness sensor is installed, wet hours are estimated from rain and near-saturated humidity, and labelled as an estimate.

3 · Crop stage

Stage from satellite

NDVI and, later, Sentinel-1 radar give the crop stage: transplanting, tillering, panicle, ripening. Each pest is scored only in the stages when it does damage.

4 · Rules

Count the criteria met

For each pest, count how many cited weather criteria the coming week meets, as in the NCIPM rules ([1]): more than three of five = high, three = moderate.

5 · Satellite check

NDVI anomaly flag

Separately, a plot is flagged when its NDVI falls below its own recent trend, or below nearby cropland on the same date, outside the normal harvest decline. That flag means "go and look", not a diagnosis. Satellite-observed

6 · On-farm, later

Sensors, traps and cameras

An on-plot weather station and leaf-wetness sensor replace the modelled inputs. Pheromone traps, with a camera counting moths, give the stem-borer and leaf-folder counts that NRRI's 4–5 moths-per-trap threshold needs [3].

Alerts point to official advice. When a risk is high, the alert links the latest ICAR-NRRI advisory and the IMD district agromet advisory for Koraput [3] [9]. FarmBot will not suggest its own pesticide doses. Check with the local agriculture extension officer before any spraying.
