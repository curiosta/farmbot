This week's advice

  • Harvest: not yet. The last clear satellite picture (23 Oct) still showed a green crop (greenness 0.89). Rough harvest window 15 Nov–05 Dec (estimate from last year plus radar planting dates; planted about 3–4 weeks later than last year (radar), so likely in the later part of the window). Start checking ears from about 10 Nov: harvest when 80–85% of grains are straw-coloured and hard.
  • Rain: heavy-rain days expected Wed 04 Nov (78 mm), Thu 05 Nov (91 mm). Before them, open the field outlets so low corners drain; do not cut, thresh or dry grain outdoors on rainy days (IMD advice).
  • Road-side strip: the plot has the Panchayat pond just across the road on its north side. After the heavy rain, walk the strip next to the road and check it drains; standing water there can delay cutting.
  • Storms/wind: if plants fall over, tie 4–5 hills together so ears stay off the water (IMD advice).
  • Drier days in the forecast (under 2 mm rain a day), useful for field work: 10 Nov–13 Nov (4 days).
  • Check for Blast (HIGH weather risk): Walk the field and look at the neck just below the ear: a grey-brown ring there (neck blast) makes the ear go white and empty. Also look for eye-shaped spots with grey centres on leaves. If you find it, ask KVK Koraput or the agriculture officer what to do before buying any spray.
  • Keep an eye on Sheath blight (watch): Part the plants and look at the stems near the water line for grey-green oval patches with brown edges.
  • After harvest: dry grain in the sun to about 14% moisture before storing; 12% if keeping it as seed (NRRI advice).

Field checks

What to look for in the field

The alerts only become trustworthy for Koraput once they are compared with what is actually seen in the fields. A simple weekly note (date, field, count) is enough. One season of notes would let us test the rules.

Stem borer and leaf folder mothsA light trap (a bulb over a pan of water) by the field, or 4-5 pheromone traps per acre (lure traps sold for "yellow stem borer" and "leaf folder"). Count the moths each morning and add them up for the week.4-5 male moths in one pheromone trap (NRRI). Why these levels are untested

The insect rules were worked out from light-trap counts at Raipur and other places, and the disease rules from research write-ups. None has been checked in Koraput's hill climate, so the levels may warn too little or too much. Treat a high level as "go and look", not as proof of a pest.

How the check works

From weather to a level, step by step

1 · Weather

Forecast and recent weather

Hourly temperature, humidity, rain, wind and sunshine at each followed plot, from Open-Meteo Estimate. These become the weekly values the rules use.

2 · Wet leaves

Hours the leaves stay wet

Blast needs leaves to stay wet for more than 10 hours [2]. Until a leaf-wetness sensor is put in, wet hours are estimated from rain and very damp air.

3 · Crop stage

Crop stage

For the followed plots, the greenness score and the radar satellite give the crop's stage. For the rest of the village, find your own stage in the table at the top. Each pest is only checked at the stages when it does damage.

4 · Rules

Count the signs

For each pest, count how many of the listed weather signs the week meets, as the pest centre's rules do [1].

5 · Plan for 2027 · nothing installed

Sensors, traps and cameras

A rain gauge, smell-bait traps for stem borer moths and small solar light traps would replace the estimates with real counts [3]. Starter set about Rs 9,100–16,600. See the plan →

Official advice first. The levels point to the latest advice from the National Rice Research Institute and the India Meteorological Department (the weather office) for Koraput [3] [9]. FarmBot does not suggest pesticide doses.
