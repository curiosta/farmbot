Harvest heads-up

FarmBot watches each plot's greenness fall as the grain ripens and the field turns golden (ripening). Every photo since 23 Oct has been cloudy, so there is no newer reading.

Rough harvest window: about 15 Nov – 5 Dec 2026 (an estimate, not a forecast). Last year both plots went from green to bare between about 10 and 22 Nov 2025. This year the Temple plot was planted earlier, so it is likely ready first. The Fishing Pond plot was planted about 3–4 weeks later than last year, so it is likely in the later part of the window. Check the grain: harvest when 80–85% of grains are straw-coloured and hard.

Fishing Pond plot: a possible weak patch of about 0.21 ha, mostly in the south-west part. Far more of the plot was weak in both years than chance alone would give. Worth a walk to look for poor soil, standing water or pests there.

Temple plot: no weak patch. No part of it was clearly weaker in both years.

Radar that sees through clouds Seen by satellite

A radar satellite passes about every 12 days and sees through monsoon cloud. Standing water on a paddy field makes the radar echo dark; it gets brighter again as the rice grows. So the darkest pass in the monsoon shows roughly when the field was flooded for transplanting. Dates are good to about ±12 days, one pass either way.

What this changes

  • Planting was in August, not June. The Fishing Pond plot was planted mid-to-late August 2026 and the Temple plot around mid-August 2026 (radar estimates, give or take 12 days). The green seen from the photo satellite in May–June was most likely grass or weeds on the empty fields.
  • The Fishing Pond plot was planted about 3–4 weeks later than last year. That is why it looks behind last year, and why its harvest is likely later.

Does the road-side strip of the Fishing Pond plot stay wetter?

The Panchayat Pond lies just across the road on the plot's north side. FarmBot compared the radar echo of the road-side part of the plot, nearest that pond, with the rest of the plot.

No sign that it stays wetter or waterlogged longer. On most passes the road-side strip looked a little drier than the rest (it looked wetter on only 31% of passes). After heavy rain (30 mm or more in 3 days) the gap narrowed, a weak hint that the strip holds some extra water then. In 2026 both parts were flooded for planting on the same pass, and the strip was growing again first. Trees and the road along that edge may make the radar look brighter there, so this check can't rule out short-lived puddles.

Estimate · assumed depth, not measured

How far could the Panchayat Pond stretch to a winter crop?

After the paddy harvest, a short winter crop such as green gram (moong), black gram (biri) or mustard could follow on the Fishing Pond plot. The only water source counted here is the Panchayat Pond: the small road-side pond just across the road, north of the plot. It belongs to the local Panchayat, and water is already drawn from it for the plot. The village fishing pond's water can't be used, so it is not counted. This works out how far that same pond could stretch to a winter crop. It counts the water each crop uses (from drying by sun and wind and the crop's stage), the little winter rain that helps, water lost on the way to the plants, the water the pond itself loses to the sun, and a share kept back for the water already being drawn. Sowing is taken as 1 December, after the harvest.

What to know first

  • The depth is assumed, not measured: 1.5 m, about as deep as a typical swimming pool. Nobody has measured the pond yet. The depth table also shows 1.0 m and 2.0 m. A marked pole would settle it.
  • Water already used: assumed to take about 25% of what the pond holds after its own drying. This is a guess; the real share is not known.
  • The pond is small and mostly covered by weeds. Only 0.25 ha of its 0.81 ha outline showed open water on 23 Oct 2026.
  • Clearing some weeds is an option to get more usable water. The most open water seen in the last year (its fullest) was 0.40 ha.

What this says

  • At the assumed 1.5 m, the pond could water about 0.63–0.70 ha of black gram or green gram from today's open water. That is roughly a quarter of the 2.49 ha plot. Longer mustard needs much more water: about 0.32 ha.
  • If the pond is only 1.0 m deep, that falls to about 0.38–0.43 ha, because the pond loses about 22–24 cm to the sun over the winter whatever you do.
  • Clearing some weeds helps. At its fullest open water, 1.5 m would water about 1.13 ha of green gram. Water under the weeds is not seen by the satellite, so the real amount is not known.
  • The open water shrinks in winter. Last December it was only about 0.10 ha, less than half of today's.
  • Winter rain hardly helps. Over December–February the middle winter had about 41 mm of rain (Open-Meteo) or 17 mm (NASA), over 25 winters, and most of it is too light to reach the roots.

The depth and the water already used are assumptions, so the real figure may differ. What could change it.

Sources and method (for specialists)

Crop water use = crop factor × reference drying (FAO-56 single crop coefficient), stages 20/30/30/20% of the season. Useful rain per 30 days by the FAO method. Pond loss = 1.05 × reference drying minus rain. Hectares = open water × (assumed depth − pond loss) × (1 − 0.25 already used) ÷ water to pump per hectare. Open water = water score above 0 inside the traced pond outline on cloud-free Sentinel-2 photos (102 photos, 1 Oct 2025 to 23 Oct 2026). Weather: Open-Meteo daily archive at the Fishing Pond plot, winters 2001–2025; normal = middle winter, dry = the winter that only 1 in 5 years is worse than. Compare with neighbouring fields

On its own, a plot can't tell a bad season from a weak field. So FarmBot compares each plot with the paddy fields within about 2 km, this October against last October. If every field dropped, the season was poor; if only the followed plot dropped, look at that plot. Fields are described only by direction and distance. No owners.

The short answer

  • Fishing Pond plot: about the same as its neighbours. It sits right in the middle of the neighbouring paddy. Plan for the 2027 paddy season · nothing installed yet

Sensors, traps and cameras: what to put where

Today every number on this site comes from satellites and computer weather. A few cheap things on the ground would check them. This is a plan only. Nothing has been bought or installed. Places come from what the satellite found: the weak south-west part of the Fishing Pond plot, the Panchayat Pond across the road, and its unknown depth.

A cheap starter set: about Rs 7,850–14,650

One water-depth ruler for the Panchayat Pond, one rain gauge, smell-bait (pheromone) traps for both plots, two small solar light traps and two home-made field water tubes. None needs mains electricity or a phone network: someone reads them and sends a phone photo each week. The 4G camera, soil wetness meter and full weather station are extras for later. Power and 4G

  • Only the camera needs a phone network. Check that a Jio or Airtel 4G signal reaches the south-west corner of the Fishing Pond plot with a phone before buying one. A camera also needs a monthly data plan.
  • The light traps and the camera carry their own small solar panels and batteries, so no wiring to the fields is needed.
  • Use the light traps for counting, not as a cure: they also catch friendly insects. Ask KVK Koraput before using traps to control pests.
