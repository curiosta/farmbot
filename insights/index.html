FarmBot watches each plot's greenness fall as the grain ripens and the field turns golden (ripening). Every photo since 23 Oct has been cloudy, so there is no newer reading.

Rough harvest window: about 15 Nov – 5 Dec 2026 (an estimate, not a forecast). Last year both plots went from green to bare between about 10 and 22 Nov 2025. Is this year's crop better or worse than last year's? Experimental

Satellites can't weigh grain. They can show how green the crop was compared with last season, as a yield comparison score. It is a comparison, not tonnes. Technical name: relative yield index

PlotMiddle greenness, 1 Oct–23 Oct 2025Same dates, 2026Score: 2026 divided by 2025
Fishing Pond plot0.84 (0.75–0.90, 6 photos)0.75 (0.59–0.89, 10 photos)0.89
Temple plot0.79 (0.71–0.85, 6 photos)0.87 (0.68–0.95, 8 photos)1.10

How to read this: the last column is the score. 1.00 means as green as last year. Above 1 is greener, below 1 is less green. The small numbers are the lowest and highest photo, and how many photos were used.

What this does and doesn't show

  • The Fishing Pond plot looks a little behind last year (score 0.89), mostly because it was planted later. A fair comparison is possible only after harvest. Radar shows it was flooded for planting around 24 Aug 2026, about 3–4 weeks later than in 2025, so on 1–23 October its crop was younger.
  • The Temple plot was greener than last October (1.10).
  • Only 1–23 October can be compared, because the photos start on 1 Oct 2025. A peak-against-peak comparison will be done once this season's greenest point is known.

Also planned · B

Weak patches inside a plot Experimental

Each 10 m square fully inside the outline (leaving out the edge row) is compared with the plot's middle value on the same day, using the cloud-free photos of 1–23 October in 2025 and 2026. A square in the lowest 20% in both years could be a weak patch. About 4% of squares would land there by chance.

What it shows

Fishing Pond plot: a possible weak patch. 21 squares (about 0.21 ha) were in the lowest 20% in both years. That is 11% of the squares tested, almost three times what chance would give. Most are in the south-west part of the plot. The year-to-year match score is 0.34: a weak but real pattern. Worth a walk to look for poor soil, standing water or pests there.

Temple plot: no weak patch. Only 14 squares (2%) were low in both years, fewer than chance. Match score -0.32.

The earlier weak strip (old snapshot) came from a rough 600 m square around the fishing pond. None of its 127 squares is inside the plot; they are about 195 m away on average.

Where to put sensors: one soil-wetness sensor in the weaker south-west part of the Fishing Pond plot and one near its middle would show whether wetness explains the difference.

Also planned · C

Radar that sees through clouds Seen by satellite

A radar satellite passes about every 12 days and sees through monsoon cloud. Standing water on a paddy field makes the radar echo dark; it gets brighter again as the rice grows. So the darkest pass in the monsoon shows roughly when the field was flooded for transplanting. Dates are good to about ±12 days, one pass either way.

PlotFlooded for planting, 2025Flooded for planting, 2026Crop growing again by (2026)
Fishing Pond plotabout 29 Jul (±12 days)about 24 Aug (±12 days)29 Sep
Temple plotabout 29 Jul (±12 days, weak dip)about 12 Aug (±12 days)5 Sep

What this changes

  • Planting was in August, not June. The Fishing Pond plot was planted mid-to-late August 2026 and the Temple plot around mid-August 2026 (radar estimates, give or take 12 days). The green seen from the photo satellite in May–June was most likely grass or weeds on the empty fields.
  • The Fishing Pond plot was planted about 3–4 weeks later than last year. That is why it looks behind last year, and why its harvest is likely later.

Does the road-side strip of the Fishing Pond plot stay wetter?

The plot has a second pond just across the road on its north side. FarmBot compared the radar echo of the part of the plot within 50 m of that pond (60 squares) with the rest of the plot (172 squares).

No sign that it stays wetter or waterlogged longer. On most passes the road-side strip looked a little drier than the rest (it looked wetter on only 30% of passes). After heavy rain (30 mm or more in 3 days) the gap narrowed, a weak hint that the strip holds some extra water then. In 2026 both parts were flooded for planting on the same pass, and the strip was growing again first. Trees and the road along that edge may make the radar look brighter there, so this check can't rule out short-lived puddles. Experimental

Is there enough pond water for a winter crop? Experimental

Could the fishing pond water a low-water winter crop such as green gram, black gram or mustard? This compares the water in the pond with what a crop would need after the little winter rain.

1.30 ha

Middle fishing-pond water area in November 2025 (10 cloud-free photos, water score). It was 0.99 ha in March–May 2026. Seen by satellite

471 − 20 mm

Water pulled out by sun and wind, minus rain, from 1 Nov 2025 to 28 Feb 2026. That leaves a shortfall of 451 mm that a crop would need from the pond. Estimate From Open-Meteo.

≈ 2.9 ha per metre

Each metre of usable depth over 1.30 ha holds about 13,000 cubic metres of water. At that drying rate, it could water about 2.9 ha of crop.

Big unknowns

  • Nobody knows how deep the pond is. The answer grows with the usable depth, so it has to be measured, for example with a marked pole.
  • The second pond across the road is a possible backup source, but the satellite saw only 0.25–0.40 ha of open water there (its outline is 0.81 ha). Most of it seems covered by weeds or reeds.
  • The figure doesn't yet include each crop's real needs, losses in the field, water lost from the pond itself, or what the fish need. These will lower the hectares.
  • If the Fishing Pond plot is harvested in late November or early December, a winter crop there would start correspondingly later.
